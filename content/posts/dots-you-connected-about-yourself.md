A kid walks into his dad's bedroom. Dad is lying in bed, as usual. Dad is tired, as usual. The kid asks dad to go to the park and play football. Dad farts, laughs, and tells the kid to go away.

A few years later the same kid, now about seven, saves up his pocket money and buys the girl he likes a Valentine's card and a Mars bar. He gets down on one knee at school (he saw it on a TV ad). She says, *"No. You stink."*

A bit later, picked last for football. Doesn't know how to play. Dad never took him.

A bit later, fails an exam. Then another.

A bit later, rejected by the army on his first application.

Each of those events, on its own, is just a thing that happened. Not a verdict on him. Not a verdict on anyone. Dads get tired. Seven-year-olds say cruel things because they are seven. Exam results are exam results. The army rejects a lot of people.

But he was a child. He was a **meaning-making machine**, which is what all of us are. And when he had two of these events, he started to feel the shape of a pattern. By three, the pattern was undeniable. By five, it had a name.

The name was: *"I am not good enough."*

That sentence then ran the next twenty-five years of his life. Not the events. The sentence.

---

## The actual mechanism

This is how almost every limiting belief you have about yourself was built. It isn't mysterious. There is a specific pattern.

```
1 event:  noise. Nothing to interpret.

2 events (similar): coincidence? Starts asking,
                    "what does this mean?"

3+ events (similar): pattern. You now have a story.
                     The story becomes a verdict.
                     The verdict runs you.
```

The upgrade from "events" to "pattern" to "verdict about who I am" happens inside your head, usually before you're old enough to spot it happening. By the time you can spot it, the verdict feels like reality instead of like a story you authored.

**The events were real. The verdict was invented.** This is the single most useful distinction in this whole area. Everything in your past that you still carry is a verdict, not a set of events. The events happened. The verdict is editorial.

---

## The timeline that quietly runs your life

```
PAST            PRESENT          FUTURE
(experiences)   (where you       (expectations)
                 live)

Each verdict    Shapes how you   And projects
you built       feel today       what you expect
back there -->  about today  --> tomorrow to be
```

The present is where you actually are. But the present is almost never experienced neutrally. It is coloured by the verdicts you brought with you from the past, and by the expectations those verdicts generate about the future.

So two people can be standing in the exact same situation and have completely different experiences of it. Same facts. Same room. Same day. Different timelines running inside their heads.

---

## What trauma, fear and anxiety actually are, in this frame

**Trauma is clinging to a past event in the present.** The event happened. It is not happening now. But you are reliving it emotionally in the present as if it is. The body does not know the difference. You are suffering today what already finished happening years ago.

**Fear and anxiety are the opposite move.** You are reliving in advance a future event that has not happened yet, projecting the shape of past events onto a future that has not been written.

You can not actually be traumatised by something that is not happening right now. You can only be traumatised by your mind replaying a past event in the present. **Likewise, you cannot be anxious about something that hasn't happened yet.** You can only be anxious about your mind rehearsing a future that it has invented, usually based on the pattern it noticed in your past.

Both are the same move, just pointed in different directions:

```
TRAUMA     =  past event, replayed now
ANXIETY    =  future event, pre-played now

Both run on the same timeline distortion:
you aren't actually here, you're there.
```

---

## The way out: separate the events from the verdict

The events in your past are facts. You cannot change them. They happened.

The verdict is a story you wrote about those events, usually when you were young, usually without noticing, almost always wrong.

The move is to **put the two in different columns**.

```
EVENTS                              VERDICT I WROTE ABOUT THEM
---------------------------------   ---------------------------------
Dad didn't play with me             "I am not worth
when I asked.                        spending time on."

Someone I liked rejected me         "I am not likeable."
badly.

Didn't make the football team       "I'm not good at anything
in gym class.                        people want."

Failed two exams.                   "I'm not smart enough."

Rejected by the thing I wanted      "I'm not good enough,
to join on my first try.             full stop."

----
These happened.                     This was never anywhere
These are facts.                    except inside my own head.
                                    I wrote this. I can rewrite it.
```

The exercise, when you're ready, is to do this with your own list. Not a short list. A long one. Write every event in your past that you still feel the sting of. In one column, just what happened. In another column, the story you spun from it.

Looking at those two columns, side by side, is almost always the first time you see the gap. The gap is where you've been living.

---

## The hard part

Noticing that your verdict about yourself is a story doesn't make the story go away. It has been running your life for a long time. It has grooved itself in deep. It feels like reality.

But seeing it is still the first move. **You cannot rewrite a story you can't see.** Once you can see "I'm not good enough" as a *sentence you wrote*, instead of a *fact about you*, it loses a specific kind of grip. It becomes a thing you can disagree with. It becomes a thing you can write a different draft of.

The rewriting is slow. The seeing is instant and free.

## Self-check

Pick one belief you hold about yourself that quietly runs things. One sentence long. *"I'm the kind of person who..."* or *"I'm just not good at..."* or *"I always..."*.

Then:

```
What's the sentence?

__________________________________________________

What are 3 or 4 events from your past that feel
like evidence for it?

1. _________________________________________
2. _________________________________________
3. _________________________________________
4. _________________________________________

If you handed those events to a stranger with no
other information, would they arrive at the same
sentence?

__________________________________________________
```

The last question is the one that does the work. Almost nobody would arrive at your sentence from your evidence. The evidence doesn't actually support the verdict. The verdict exists because *you* were the one standing inside the events, feeling them, and a scared seven-year-old (or ten-year-old, or thirteen-year-old) did the writing.

That writer is still inside you, holding the pen. You can take the pen now.

---

## The one line

If you keep nothing else from this post, keep this:

> The events in your past are facts. The sentence you wrote about them is not. You wrote it. You can rewrite it.

Your life story is not what happened to you. It is what you decided those things meant. The deciding happened a long time ago, often by a small version of you who did not have the information or the perspective to decide well. **The grown version of you is allowed to look at the same events and decide differently.** That is the whole project, and it starts with seeing that there was ever any deciding at all.
