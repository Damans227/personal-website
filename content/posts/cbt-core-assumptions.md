I came across a lecture slide on **Cognitive Behavior Therapy (CBT)** that lists seven core assumptions the whole approach is built on. CBT is one of the most studied forms of talk therapy, and these seven lines are basically the operating system underneath it. They are worth sitting with even if you never see a therapist, because they quietly reframe how you think about your own mind.

Here is the list, in my own words, with what I think each one is really saying.

---

## 1. You are a processor of experience, not a passive victim of circumstance

This is the one everything else sits on top of. Two very different mental models of a person:

```
VICTIM MODEL                          PROCESSOR MODEL
---------------                       ----------------
Life happens TO me.                   Life happens, and I
                                      INTERPRET what it means.

My feelings are the direct            My feelings follow my
result of events.                     interpretation, not the
                                      event itself.

If the event changes,                 If my interpretation
my feelings change.                   changes, my feelings can
                                      change too, even if the
                                      event does not.
```

CBT starts here. Between the thing that happened and how you feel about it, there is a step. You are the one doing that step. Which means you have more power in the chain than it feels like you do when you are hurting.

---

## 2. You are not broken, you just need to be understood

A lot of self-help language is secretly in the "broken, needs fixing" frame. CBT rejects that. Nobody showed up as a defective product. People develop patterns that stop working for them, and the job is to understand how those patterns got installed, not to tear the person apart and rebuild them.

The practical difference is huge. "Fixing" starts from shame. "Understanding" starts from curiosity. One of them is sustainable. The other one burns out.

---

## 3. If you learned an unhelpful pattern, you can unlearn it just as fast

This is the hopeful one, and it is also the one most people doubt. If anxiety or avoidance or self-criticism were learned, often very young, they can also be unlearned. Not instantly, but not glacially either. The brain that absorbed the pattern is the same brain that can replace it.

The implication is uncomfortable in a good way: **the amount of time your pattern took to show up is not a prediction of how long it will take to change.** The idea that "I've been like this my whole life so it will take my whole life to fix" is not true. It just feels true.

---

## 4. You are solely responsible for your thoughts, feelings, actions, and behavior

This one lands hard. Nobody else is in your head choosing what you think, how you react, or what you do next. Even when something bad is done to you, the response is yours.

To be clear, "responsible" does not mean "to blame." It means **in control of the next step**. Blame looks backward. Responsibility looks forward. The second one is useful. The first one just hurts.

---

## 5. You can't control the thoughts that enter your mind, but you can choose whether to act on them

This is the one I keep coming back to. The mind generates thoughts constantly, including ugly ones, intrusive ones, catastrophic ones. You did not choose them. You cannot filter them at the door.

What you can do is this:

```
         INPUT                      YOUR CHOICE
         -----                      -----------

Thought appears   -->   Notice it   -->   Act on it
                                    -->   Or let it pass
```

The gap between "a thought appeared" and "I acted on the thought" is where all of CBT lives. Train that gap, and most of the thought no longer runs your life.

---

## 6. You can choose a new set of habits, thought patterns, and behaviors

This is point 3 restated as a choice, not a possibility. The capacity to change is not the same as actually changing. The second one requires picking a new pattern, running it on purpose, and running it again when the old one tries to come back.

The honest version is that this is boring work. New patterns feel fake for a while before they feel natural. The slide quietly captures this with the caption at the bottom: *"only once they realize that they can."* The realization comes first. The practice follows. Both have to happen.

---

## 7. The meaning of your communication is determined by other people, not by you

This is the humbling one. You can intend a message perfectly. The person on the other side still gets to decide what they heard.

```
What you MEANT  -->  what you SAID  -->  what they HEARD

You control the first two.
You do not control the third.
```

That does not mean every miscommunication is your fault. It means that if you care about being understood, you are responsible for noticing the gap and adjusting, not for assuming your intent should have been enough.

In relationships, this one quietly resolves a huge number of fights. "I didn't mean it that way" is true. It is also not the end of the conversation.

---

## How to actually use this

The way I would read a list like this is not "which of these do I agree with," but **where do I actually live right now**. Go through them one at a time and ask yourself the honest version:

```
Assumption                     Where do I actually live?
----------                     -------------------------
1. Processor vs victim         Which one did I feel like today?

2. Understood vs fixed         Am I trying to understand myself,
                               or beat myself into shape?

3. Unlearning is possible      Do I believe my old pattern can go,
                               or am I secretly sure it can't?

4. Responsibility, not blame   Am I looking back (blame) or
                               forward (responsibility)?

5. Thoughts vs actions         Did I notice the gap today, or did
                               the thought just drive the car?

6. Choose new patterns         Did I actually run a new pattern
                               this week, or just think about it?

7. Communication is received   When someone misread me, did I
                               adjust, or just insist I was right?
```

That exercise is more useful than any amount of reading about CBT. The list is just a mirror. The work is in what you do when you look.
