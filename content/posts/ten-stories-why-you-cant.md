A limiting belief doesn't usually arrive wearing a sign that says "limiting belief." It arrives wearing a sign that says "obviously true." It sounds like common sense. It sounds like humility. It sounds like being realistic about yourself. It is almost never any of those things. It is almost always a story you have been telling yourself for so long that you have stopped noticing you are the one telling it.

Here are ten of the most common ones. If you can spot yourself in two or three of these, you have found the stories that are quietly running your decisions.

---

## 1. "I'm too old to start anew."

The oldest excuse there is. Also, if you take the time to look, almost never true.

The queen of England started her second (and third, and fourth) chapter in her nineties. Painters have started painting in their seventies. Entrepreneurs have founded their most successful companies in their fifties and sixties. You are not racing a clock, you are measuring yourself against an imaginary clock that nobody else is looking at.

**The real question:** too old compared to who?

---

## 2. "I'm not smart enough."

Compared to whom? Smart at what? Measured how?

"Smart" is almost always a vague, undefined word that lets you dismiss yourself without having to be specific. The honest version would be: *"I am less practised at this particular skill than I would like to be."* Which is a problem that gets fixed by practising. Not by having been born with a different brain.

**The real question:** which specific skill, and have you actually tried to develop it?

---

## 3. "I'm not skilled enough."

Of course you aren't. Nobody is, before they do the thing. Skill is what you have after you do the thing, not what qualifies you to do it.

The people whose skill you admire looked, at some point in their past, exactly like you do now. The difference is that they started before they felt ready. Nobody feels ready.

**The real question:** are you waiting to feel skilled before you start, or are you starting so that you become skilled?

---

## 4. "I couldn't handle failing."

You have failed before. Many times. You are reading this. You are still here.

What you really mean is: *"I would feel bad if I failed."* True. You would. The feeling would last a few hours or a few days. It would pass. You would still be the person reading this. The failure would not kill you.

**The real question:** what specifically are you afraid would happen? Say it out loud. Usually it collapses on contact with daylight.

---

## 5. "Money is the root of all evil."

A misquote that has been doing enormous damage for about four hundred years. The actual original line was *the love of money* is the root of evil, which is a very different claim.

Money is an enabler. It funds the things you want to do, including the generous things. The quiet consequence of holding this belief is that you refuse to let yourself have enough of it to actually help anyone. You stay broke in service of your own purity, which doesn't help you and doesn't help anyone you were hoping to serve.

**The real question:** what good thing could you do if you weren't scared of being seen as greedy?

---

## 6. "I've already tried everything."

No you haven't. You have tried a small, specific list of things, usually variations of the same approach.

Have you asked for help? Have you asked a different kind of person for help? Have you tried doing nothing for a month? Have you moved cities? Have you worked with someone one-on-one? Have you written it down? Have you deliberately done the opposite of your instinct for a week? Have you waited a year and tried again with completely different context?

**The real question:** can you list ten things you have tried, specifically? Most people cannot.

---

## 7. "I can't learn anything new."

If this were true, you would not be reading this sentence. You are learning right now.

What this sentence usually means is: *"I don't want to look like a beginner."* Learning new things requires being bad at them in public for a while. Adults are often not willing to be bad at anything in public. So they tell themselves they can't learn, instead of admitting they won't tolerate the discomfort of being new.

**The real question:** is the problem that you can't learn, or that you are not willing to be visibly a beginner?

---

## 8. "I don't deserve it."

Nobody deserves it. This is the quietly shocking thing. The people who have the lives you want did not qualify for them in advance. They just started before they felt they were allowed to.

Deservingness is a frame that exists in your head. Nobody at any gate is checking a list to see if you earned the right to be here. The whole concept is imaginary.

**The real question:** who exactly do you think is deciding whether you deserve this? If you can name them, you can argue with them. If you can't, there is nobody.

---

## 9. "I'm not as good as [someone else]."

Of course you aren't. They are them. You are you. You will never be a better version of them than they are. **You will always be a second-best version of someone else, at best.**

The flip side is also true. **You are the only expert at being you.** Nobody else can occupy that role. The question is not whether you measure up to someone else. It is whether you are willing to actually inhabit the one role you are the world's leading expert at.

**The real question:** what would you be doing differently if you stopped trying to be the other person?

---

## 10. "I don't have enough stuff yet."

The classic. I don't have enough money, enough resources, enough credentials, enough followers, enough experience, enough qualifications, enough confidence. Just one more course, one more book, one more year, and I'll be ready.

You will not be ready. You are already ready for the next step. You do not need to be ready for the final step; nobody is. **To help someone, you only need to be a couple of steps ahead of them.** You are already a couple of steps ahead of someone, right now, in something you have figured out that they are still stuck on. That is where you start.

**The real question:** who could you help right now with what you already know? Start there.

---

## The underlying mechanism

These ten sentences are not random. They are all variations of the same move: finding a reason that locates the problem *inside you*, in a way that cannot be challenged, so you don't have to try.

Each one has the same shape:

```
"I can't [thing I want to do],
 because [permanent fact about me]."
```

The permanent fact about you is almost never actually permanent. It is a belief you built in the past, resting on a few events your mind connected into a verdict. (The architecture of this is in a [separate post](/posts/dots-you-connected-about-yourself/).)

The sentence feels like reality. It is really just a story. **The way you tell it is the thing that is stopping you.** Not the facts underneath.

---

## A perspective story worth carrying

One line from a man in Haiti who lost almost everyone in the 2010 earthquake. His parents, siblings, wife, children, his home, his business. All of it. Three days later, he was helping volunteers unload medical supplies. Asked how he was functioning, how he hadn't collapsed, he said:

> *"When a man has lost everything, all that is left for a man to do is give something back."*

I think about that line a lot when I catch myself running one of the ten excuses above. Someone who has every genuine reason to be stuck chose not to be stuck. Which means the stuckness we feel over our much smaller problems is almost always a story we are telling, not a situation we are in.

## Self-check

Go back up and read the ten again, slowly. Mark the two or three that stung.

```
My top excuses:

1. _________________________________________

2. _________________________________________

3. (optional) _______________________________
```

Those are yours. Those are the stories doing the most work in your life right now.

For each one, write the sentence that follows the pattern:

```
"I can't [do X] because [permanent fact about me]."
```

Then write it a second way:

```
"I'm telling myself I can't [do X] because [the story]."
```

The two sentences describe the same situation. One of them is a cage. The other is a draft of something you can rewrite.

---

## The one line

If you keep nothing else from this post, keep this:

> A limiting belief isn't a fact about you. It's a sentence you've been telling yourself long enough to mistake it for one.

Your ten favourite excuses are not evidence of who you are. They are evidence of what you have been quietly rehearsing. **The rehearsal is why they feel true.** Catch yourself saying one, and you have already stopped reciting it on autopilot.
