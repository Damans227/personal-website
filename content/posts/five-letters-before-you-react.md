Most of the stuff you regret saying, you said in the first two seconds. That tiny gap between the trigger and your mouth is where whole relationships get cheaper, where emails get sent that cannot be unsent, where you say the thing in the voice you wish you hadn't.

There is a five-letter mnemonic for that gap. It is boring, which is its best quality. In a hot moment you do not have the brain cells to run a philosophy. You can run five letters.

**STOPP.**

---

## S: Stop

The whole move hinges on this one. Just stop. Don't say anything, don't type, don't move. Say the word to yourself if you have to. Even half a second of actually stopping is enough to break autopilot, which is what was about to run the reaction for you.

Autopilot is a very good system for brushing your teeth. It is a terrible system for responding to the thing that just lit you up.

---

## T: Take a breath

One slow in-breath. One slower out-breath. Not a ceremony. Not a yoga pose. Just one.

The reason this works is not spiritual. It is physical. A longer out-breath genuinely does something to your nervous system. The effect is modest and real. More importantly, taking the breath is a legal reason to not say anything yet, which is 90% of the point.

---

## O: Observe

Now, with the half second you just bought yourself, look at what is actually happening.

```
What am I feeling in my body right now?
What am I actually reacting to?
What is the thought that just fired?
```

Three questions. Three or four seconds. You are not solving anything yet. You are just *noticing*, which is the whole mechanism by which a reaction stops running you and starts being a thing you can see.

---

## P: Perspective

Now one small zoom-out. One of these questions is usually enough:

```
Is this fact, or is this my opinion?
Could there be another way to read this?
What would I tell a friend standing in my shoes?
Will this still matter in a week?
```

You do not need to answer all of them. One is enough to loosen the grip of the first reading. The first reading was almost certainly a distortion of some kind. (If you want the catalogue of distortions, that is a [separate post](/posts/twelve-shapes-your-thoughts-twist-into/).)

---

## P: Practise what works

Now choose. Not react. **Choose.**

```
What is the best thing I can actually do here,
for me, for the other person, for the situation?
```

The important word is *best*. Not "what feels most satisfying in this exact second," which is almost always revenge, withdrawal, or a cutting line you've been workshopping. Best. Over the next hour. Over the next week. Over the next version of this relationship you want to be in.

Then do that.

---

## The short version

Carry this one, not the full five sentences:

> **Stop. Breathe. Notice. Reframe. Choose.**

That is the whole move in five words. If STOPP ever gets lost in the moment, the five-word version will still fit in your head.

---

## How to actually use it

Do not save STOPP for the big moments. If the first time you ever try it is the middle of a serious fight, you will not remember any of it. Your brain will skip straight to the usual reaction, because that is the one it has rehearsed.

Rehearse it on the small stuff instead:

- The mildly rude person at the coffee counter.
- The email that annoyed you more than it should have.
- The small sting when someone did not reply.
- The tiny flare of impatience in traffic.

Run STOPP on those. All five letters. Even when you don't need to. **The point of the practice is not the small moment. The point is to make the reflex automatic, so it shows up when you actually need it.**

By the time something big happens, STOPP needs to be a thing your body already knows how to do. The small moments are the training ground.

---

## The one line

If you keep nothing else from this post, keep this:

> The space between the trigger and the response is the only part of this chain you actually control. Everything you want to become a better version of lives inside that space.

Everything else is just reaction. STOPP is one way, probably the simplest way, to make that space big enough to stand inside.
