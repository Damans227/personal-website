Ask someone *"who are you?"* and watch what happens. Nine times out of ten, the answer is a job title, a role, or a relationship. *"I'm a software engineer."* *"I'm a mom."* *"I'm the eldest."* *"I'm a Catholic."* *"I'm an ex-army guy."*

All true. None of it is actually the answer.

The question was *who are you*. The answer given was *what you do*. Those are different circles.

---

## Three circles

```
            +----------------------------+
            |          WHAT              |
            |   (what you do, your       |
            |    role, your rank,        |
            |    your results, your      |
            |    skills, your labels)    |
            |                            |
            |   +--------------------+   |
            |   |       HOW          |   |
            |   |  (how you feel,    |   |
            |   |   how you behave,  |   |
            |   |   how well you     |   |
            |   |   do what you do,  |   |
            |   |   how you think    |   |
            |   |   others see you)  |   |
            |   |                    |   |
            |   |   +------------+   |   |
            |   |   |    WHO     |   |   |
            |   |   | (who you   |   |   |
            |   |   |  actually  |   |   |
            |   |   |  are, with |   |   |
            |   |   |  no labels)|   |   |
            |   |   +------------+   |   |
            |   +--------------------+   |
            +----------------------------+
```

- **WHAT** is the outer ring. What you do for a living. Your rank. Your titles. Your results. Your skills. The religion you were raised in. Your preferences, your likes, your dislikes. Everything that could show up on a CV or a dating profile.
- **HOW** is the middle ring. How you feel today. How you behave. How well you did the thing. How you think other people see you.
- **WHO** is the centre. Who you actually are, with every one of those labels peeled off.

Most people live in the outer two rings and never visit the centre. They define themselves by what they do and how well they're doing it. **The equation they're secretly running is:**

> `what + how well = who`

Do the thing well → you're a good person. Do the thing badly → you're a bad person. Lose the thing entirely → you don't know who you are at all.

---

## The problem with the equation

The equation works beautifully right up until the day something in the outer ring moves. And things in the outer ring are always moving.

- You get laid off. The *"I'm a software engineer"* identity goes down with the job.
- The company reorganises and your role shrinks. The confidence shrinks with it.
- Your kids grow up and leave. The *"I'm a mom"* identity has nowhere to point.
- You retire. The thing you built your sense of self on for forty years is gone in a day.
- Your preferences change. You spent a decade identifying as X, and now you want Y, and your mind can't reconcile that you are still you.
- You get sick. You can't do the thing. The *"I'm a runner / lifter / performer"* identity goes dark.

If your WHO is built on your WHAT, every one of these events isn't an inconvenience. It's an identity collapse. The thing propping you up is gone, so *you* feel gone.

**This is why people in high-identity roles often fall apart when the role ends.** Soldiers who hand back the ID card. Athletes who retire. Executives who retire. Parents whose last child leaves. The role itself was fine. The problem was it had been doing the quiet work of answering *who am I* for twenty years, and now nothing's answering it.

---

## Capped labels vs uncapped ones

Here is the useful distinction. Some labels are **capped**. They have an off switch somewhere out in the world, and when it flips, the label is gone.

```
CAPPED LABELS (can be taken from you)
  soldier         (ends when you leave)
  CEO             (ends when the board votes)
  mom-of-littles  (ends when they grow up)
  runner          (ends when the knee goes)
  the smart one   (ends in the room with smarter people)
  the funny one   (ends on the bad day)
  Catholic        (ends when the doubt lands)
  straight / gay  (ends if your attractions shift)
```

Other labels are **uncapped**. Nobody can take them from you because they are not held by any external circumstance. They are held by how you choose to show up.

```
UNCAPPED LABELS (nobody can take from you)
  honest
  generous
  curious
  loyal
  kind
  creative
  patient
  someone who tells the truth
  someone who finishes what they start
  someone who looks after people
```

You can be honest without a job. You can be generous without money. You can be curious without a credential. You can be kind in a hospital bed. **Uncapped labels don't need the outer ring to be intact.**

The identity trap is building your WHO out of capped labels. The identity fix is noticing which uncapped labels are actually yours, and letting those be the answer.

---

## This is not the same as having "no identity"

A fair pushback: *"If I strip away all my roles and labels, I'm nothing. The labels ARE me."*

The labels are things *about* you. They are not you. The test is simple: imagine losing one of them tomorrow. If losing it feels like losing a piece of equipment, it was a label. If losing it feels like losing yourself, you were running the equation.

The person who's grounded in their WHO can lose the job and be sad about losing the job without feeling like they've lost themselves. The person who's running `what + how well = who` can't tell the difference between the two losses. **That's the whole thing this post is about.**

## A short exercise

Give yourself ten minutes. Pen and paper.

```
STEP 1
List five things from your WHAT ring. Role, title,
relationship, skill, affiliation. The things you'd
normally reach for if someone asked "tell me about
yourself."

STEP 2
For each one, ask: could this be taken from me, by
circumstances I don't fully control? If yes, write
CAPPED next to it.

STEP 3
Now, separately: write five uncapped words that
describe how you actually show up in the world
when nobody is watching. (Not aspirational. Actual.
What are you, when the room is empty.)

STEP 4
Those five uncapped words are a draft of your WHO.
Carry them for a week. Notice when you act in line
with them, and when you don't.
```

If you can't come up with five uncapped words, that's useful information too. It means you've been living in the outer ring for a long time, and the inner ring is quieter than it should be. That is fixable. You start by choosing one.

---

## The one line

If you keep nothing else from this post, keep this:

> If your sense of who you are can be deleted by a single phone call from HR, it was built in the wrong ring.

The uncapped version of you is already in there. Everything the outer ring takes away, it leaves standing.
