In an [earlier post](/posts/processor-not-victim/) I wrote about the mental shifts underneath a healthier way of being in your own head. This one is the next layer down: a map of **where a person actually sits** at any given moment, and how they move from being stuck at the bottom of the ladder to flourishing at the top.

It is a seven-rung ladder. At the bottom is the most disempowered a human can get. At the top is the state in which creativity and warmth and clarity just flow. Nobody lives permanently at one rung. You will find yourself on different rungs in different areas of your life. The point of knowing the ladder is to catch where you are honestly, and then climb one rung at a time.

---

## The whole ladder

```
RUNG   STANCE             FEELING STATE
-----  -----------------  ----------------------------------
  7    Non-Judgement      Raw passion, pure creativity
  6    Synthesis          Inner joy, innate wisdom
  5    Reconciliation     Inner peace, acceptance
  4    Concern            Compassion, service to others
  3    Responsibility     Forgiveness, cooperation
  2    Conflict           Anger, defiance
  1    Victim             Apathy, indifference
```

Each rung is a prerequisite for the one above it. You cannot skip. If you try to live two rungs higher than you really are, you will get found out, usually by the people closest to you.

Let me walk through them.

---

## 1. Victim: apathy and indifference

Life happens *to* this person. Circumstances are to blame. Other people are to blame. The system is to blame. Change is not available because the ability to change was never on offer.

The emotional signature is apathy. Not anger, because anger would at least require caring about something. Just a flat, dulled "it is what it is." Victimhood can also quietly pay. There are areas of life where staying in the victim stance gets you looked after, gets you off the hook, gets you sympathy. Which is why it is so sticky. The payoff is real.

You can be at rung 1 in only one area of your life and functional everywhere else. "I am just bad with money." "I have always been like this with relationships." The sentence that gives it away is **"there's nothing I can do."** When you catch yourself saying that, you are standing on rung 1 of that area.

---

## 2. Conflict: anger and defiance

Something inside you starts to object to being stuck. You want to change but you do not know how. You do not even know what "fixed" would look like. So you get angry.

This stage looks ugly from the outside, but it is actually a huge upgrade. The person has moved from "nothing I can do" to "I don't want this anymore." That is enormous. The anger is just the energy of change with nowhere to go yet.

```
STUCK                     IN CONFLICT
--------                  -----------
"Nothing I can do."       "I don't want this anymore,
                           but I don't know how yet."

Apathy.                   Anger. Defiance.

Payoff: sympathy,         Pain: the people closest to
protection, no demands.   you often become the bad guys
                          while you figure it out.
```

Teenagers often live here. So do people going through a transition the world does not yet see. If the people in your life are suddenly "the problem," check whether you have actually just climbed to rung 2. The anger is pointed outward, but the source is inward.

---

## 3. Responsibility: forgiveness and cooperation

This is the first rung where real work starts, and it is the one most people avoid longest.

Responsibility means owning the next step. It does not mean accepting the blame for how you got here. Blame looks backward. Responsibility looks forward. The second one is useful. The first one just hurts.

Two things have to happen on this rung:

- **Forgiving the imperfect people who made you,** so that you can stop expecting a perfect inheritance.
- **Forgiving yourself for being imperfect,** so that you can start acting without needing to be perfect first.

The reason most people stall here is that they confuse "I am responsible" with "I have to get it right." You do not. You have to start. Making a small decision, being wrong, finding out you did not die, and then making another decision. That is the loop that moves you up. Thomas Edison famously built about a thousand lightbulbs that did not work before he built one that did. The willingness to be wrong a thousand times in a row is what responsibility actually looks like.

Once you are reliably on this rung in some area, that area starts to compound. Money, health, relationships, work. The areas you fully own are the ones that start to work.

---

## 4. Concern: compassion and service to others

Here is the first big trap I want to flag.

The move from rung 3 to rung 4 is the move from "I own my own life" to "I now have real capacity to care about yours." That is a beautiful move. But it only works in that order. Trying to care for others before you have taken responsibility for yourself produces a very recognisable mess.

You see this all the time. People who want to help, who clearly care, but whose own life is quietly on fire. The help has an edge to it. They need the person they are helping to improve, because the improvement is actually how they feel okay about themselves.

An honest test: **can you serve this person well even if they never change?** If the answer is yes, you are on rung 4. If the answer is no, you are still on an earlier rung, dressed up as a helper.

The healthy version of this rung is compassion that comes from genuine understanding. You can only understand someone else as far as you have understood yourself. The deeper you have gone with your own mess, the more you can actually sit with somebody else's.

---

## 5. Reconciliation: inner peace and acceptance

Reconciliation sits at the opposite end of the ladder from conflict. Conflict is war with yourself. Reconciliation is peace with yourself.

Specifically, peace with the imperfect, flawed, inconsistent, slightly embarrassing version of you that actually exists. Not the version you want to be. The one that is here.

This rung is the quiet one. Nobody applauds reconciliation. It does not look like anything from the outside. But it changes everything that comes next. A person who has made peace with themselves does not need other people to behave in any particular way in order to feel okay. That one shift resolves most relationship problems before they start.

The classic framing of what a healthy helping relationship requires is older than I am (Carl Rogers put it on paper in the 1940s). It comes down to three things:

- **Unconditional acceptance** of the other person, including their flaws.
- **Congruence,** which means your words and your actions match. You are the same person inside and outside.
- **Empathy,** the capacity to genuinely understand where the other person is standing.

You can only offer any of these to another person if you have first offered them to yourself. Unconditional acceptance of someone else is impossible if you cannot stand your own flaws. Congruence is impossible if you are hiding something from yourself. Empathy only reaches as far as the honesty you have done with your own experience.

So this rung is the quiet infrastructure that makes rung 4 work for real, not as performance.

---

## 6. Synthesis: inner joy and innate wisdom

Synthesis sounds complicated but is actually simple. After enough time at peace with yourself, understanding stops being an effort. Things just click. The patterns that used to require thought now reveal themselves in the moment.

This is what people usually call wisdom, and the honest version of it is just time plus honesty. You have spent long enough looking at the pattern that you no longer need to look at it. It is in you.

The signature on this rung is a kind of quiet joy. Not peak-moment happiness. A baseline. Days feel lighter even when they are hard, because the war with yourself that used to tax everything is no longer running in the background. The energy that used to go into self-defence is now free.

One line that sticks with me: at this rung you no longer *need* anything for yourself, even though you still *want* plenty. There is a huge difference. Needs run you. Wants are things you move toward on your own schedule. Dropping the needs is what makes this rung possible.

---

## 7. Non-Judgement: raw passion, pure creativity

The top rung is the strangest one to describe because it is defined by the absence of something. The absence of the voice that beats you up. The absence of the voice that keeps score. The absence of the voice that measures everything against what it should have been.

What is left when that voice goes quiet is **raw passion**. Not frantic enthusiasm. Just whatever your actual, uncensored energy is, flowing without obstruction. For some people it is creativity. For some it is care. For some it is play. For some it is work. For everyone it is them, without the layer of self-attack.

Almost nobody lives here full-time. But most people have touched it. The hour where the writing just flowed, the conversation where you were fully present, the stretch at work where the solution appeared without strain. Those are rung-7 moments. The ladder is just the explanation for why they are rare: six rungs of unfinished work sit between you and that state most of the time.

---

## The real pattern: each rung is a prerequisite for the next

Reading the ladder, it is tempting to pick where you want to be and try to live there. It does not work.

```
Trying to live at rung 7 without the lower rungs
produces a performance of raw passion that collapses
the moment life stops cooperating.

Trying to serve others at rung 4 without taking
responsibility at rung 3 produces helpers who need
their people to improve.

Trying to take responsibility at rung 3 without first
passing through conflict at rung 2 produces guilt-driven
hustle that burns out.
```

The ladder is monotonic. You go through the rungs you are going to go through, in order. Skipping is not available. Which is good news, actually, because it means that **wherever you are stuck right now is the only place you can grow from.**

---

## You are at different rungs in different areas

Nobody has a single ladder for their whole life. You have one per area. Money, health, work, your closest relationships, your parenting, your creative life, your relationship with your own body. Each one is its own ladder.

You might be at rung 6 with your work and rung 1 with your health. At rung 5 with your friends and rung 2 with your family. The point of the ladder is not to score yourself as a human. It is to **sort your life by rung** and figure out which area you are going to climb one step on this week.

---

## One last thing

The ladder is not a hierarchy of people. It is a hierarchy of stances. Rung 7 does not make you better than someone at rung 2. It just means you have climbed further in a particular area.

The person at rung 2, lashing out while they figure out what they want, is doing something harder than they look like they are doing. They have left rung 1. That is the hardest move on the ladder, and most people never make it. Treat them kindly. The ladder is forgiving of where you have been. It only cares what rung you are willing to try next.
