Here is a thing scientists do that most people never do with their own minds. When a scientist comes up with a theory, their next move is not to confirm it. Their next move is to **try to break it**. They design experiments specifically to show their own theory wrong. If the theory survives repeated honest attempts to destroy it, it earns a bit more trust. If it fails, they drop it and reach for a better one.

That is not what people do with their own beliefs. People find a belief, decide it feels true, and then spend the rest of their life collecting evidence that confirms it, ignoring evidence that doesn't, and defending it when challenged. The belief then runs their life for decades, unexamined.

A better posture, borrowed from how science actually works, is to **treat each of your beliefs like a theory that needs to be stress-tested**. Not because beliefs are bad. Because any belief that cannot survive honest challenge was not worth having in the first place.

---

## The cycle beliefs actually move through

In 1962, a philosopher of science called Thomas Kuhn wrote a book called *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*. He described how scientific understanding changes over time, in a cycle:

```
NORMAL SCIENCE      The current model works. Everyone uses it.
                    Life goes on.

MODEL DRIFT         Something new comes along. The current
                    model starts to feel a little stretched.

MODEL CRISIS        The old model clearly can't account for
                    what we're now seeing. Nothing new has
                    replaced it yet. Everything is uncertain.

MODEL REVOLUTION    A new, better model takes shape. People
                    start testing it against the old one.

PARADIGM CHANGE     The new model wins. It becomes the new
                    "normal science." The cycle starts over.
```

Your beliefs run on the same cycle. All of them. The one you have about yourself, about relationships, about money, about work, about what you're capable of, about what you deserve. Each one is sitting somewhere on this wheel.

Some of them are still in the Normal Science phase, working fine, quietly serving you. Some are starting to drift because the life you have now doesn't match the belief anymore. Some are in crisis right now, which is probably why you are reading things like this. And some have already gone through a quiet revolution without you really noticing, and the new paradigm has taken over.

## The season between seasons

There's a nice image in the lecture this came from: the "fifth season."

Everyone knows there are four seasons. Spring, summer, autumn, winter. But between every pair of them, there's a secret fifth season. **Transition.** The in-between where it was supposed to be spring but there's still frost in the mornings. Or supposed to be autumn but it still feels like summer in the afternoons. The weather can't commit yet.

That is what Model Crisis feels like inside your head. The old belief doesn't fit anymore. The new one hasn't arrived yet. You are between seasons. Nothing is certain. Everything feels off.

Most people hate this phase and try to escape it by either forcing themselves back into the old belief, or grabbing any plausible new belief that happens to be nearby just to end the discomfort. Both are mistakes. **The in-between is where the actual work gets done.** Rushing through it means you end up with a belief you haven't actually tested.

## All beliefs are limiting

Here is the harder claim: **every belief you hold is limiting.** Not just the ones that obviously hurt you. All of them.

Why? Because every belief was built from your limited perspective, from the slice of experiences you happen to have had, filtered through the particular distortions your mind runs. You have never seen the world through anyone else's eyes. You have no reference frame for the beliefs you have never thought to question. So every single thing you believe is a provisional model, built on incomplete information, held with more confidence than the evidence actually supports.

That sounds grim. It isn't. It is just how it is for everyone. Scientists know this about their theories, which is why they treat them lightly, test them hard, and swap them out when something better comes along. **You can hold your beliefs the same way.**

- Lightly, not tightly.
- As models, not as truths.
- As drafts, not as verdicts.

A belief held lightly is still useful. It still guides your decisions. It just doesn't own you.

## How to stress-test a belief

Pick one. Preferably one that quietly runs something important.

```
The belief:

__________________________________________________

What are 3 pieces of evidence that would strongly
suggest this belief is wrong? (If you cannot name
any, your belief is unfalsifiable, which means it
is also unprovable, which means it does not
belong in the "truth" category at all.)

1. _________________________________________
2. _________________________________________
3. _________________________________________

In the last 12 months, have I actually seen any
of those pieces of evidence? Have I been looking?

__________________________________________________

If someone I respected said this belief is wrong,
what is the strongest argument they would make?

__________________________________________________
```

The purpose of this exercise is not to tear your belief down. It is to see whether your belief **survives honest attack.** If it does, great: hold it, use it, trust it a little more. If it doesn't, you have just saved yourself maybe a decade of operating under a story that wasn't going to hold up anyway.

## The practical move: a growth mindset is a scientific mindset

The thing people usually call a "growth mindset" is, when you look at it carefully, the scientific posture applied to yourself. The willingness to admit what you don't know. The willingness to change your mind in the face of better evidence. The willingness to drop a model that stopped working and reach for a better one.

The alternative is a fixed mindset: the belief that your current beliefs are correct, and your current limits are permanent. People with a fixed mindset stop running the cycle. They get stuck in a Normal Science phase forever, defending a model that stopped serving them, because stepping into Model Crisis is uncomfortable and uncertain.

**The whole project of growing up is being willing to live inside the cycle on purpose**, over and over, letting the old beliefs die when they stop working, sitting with the discomfort of the in-between season, and letting the new ones emerge honestly rather than desperately.

---

## The one line

If you keep nothing else from this post, keep this:

> A belief that cannot survive honest challenge was not worth having. A belief that can is worth holding lightly, because any belief that would collapse under new evidence is one you want to find out about *before* it costs you ten years.

Treat your beliefs the way scientists treat theories. **Not as true. Not as false. As provisional.** Hold them well enough to use them. Hold them loosely enough to replace them. The cycle will keep turning. The question is whether you are going to let it turn you, or go kicking and screaming.
