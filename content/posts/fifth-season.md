Everyone knows the four seasons. Spring, summer, autumn, winter. But between every pair of them, there is a quieter fifth one that nobody puts in the calendar.

**Transition.**

It's the stretch where the mornings are still frosty but the afternoons feel like spring. The weeks where it's officially autumn but you're still in a T-shirt. The light is doing something it didn't do last month. The old season is clearly over. The new season hasn't quite committed yet. Everything is undecided.

Beliefs die in that same season.

---

## How a belief actually changes

A philosopher of science called Thomas Kuhn watched how scientific understanding shifts over time, and noticed that no theory ever just dies cleanly on a Tuesday and gets replaced on Wednesday. The process has stages:

```
NORMAL        The current model works. Life goes on.
              Everyone uses it. It explains what we see.

DRIFT         Something starts not fitting. A result the
              model didn't predict. A case it can't handle.

CRISIS        The old model clearly isn't enough any more.
              Nothing new has arrived to replace it.
              Everything feels wobbly.

REVOLUTION    A new, better model takes shape.
              People try it against the old one.

PARADIGM      The new one wins. It becomes the new "normal."
              The cycle starts again.
```

Your beliefs about yourself move the same way. The belief that you're not good enough. The belief that money is for other people. The belief that nobody actually likes you. The belief that you're bad at relationships. Each of these is sitting on this wheel somewhere, right now.

Some are in Normal, running quietly in the background, dictating decisions without being examined. Some are in Drift, not fitting what your actual life looks like now, but you haven't noticed yet. Some are in Crisis, which is probably why some things feel unmoored. And some have gone through a quiet Revolution without you noticing, and the Paradigm has changed, and you now operate from a belief that your younger self would barely recognise.

---

## The important stage is Crisis

Most of what people call "stuck" is actually Crisis. The old belief has stopped working. The new belief hasn't shown up yet. **That gap is uncomfortable in a specific way: everything is uncertain, nothing is settled, and you can feel the ground is moving but you can't see where it's going to settle.**

This is the fifth season of your inner life. Not spring. Not winter. The in-between that nobody labels.

Two instincts show up here, and both are mistakes:

```
INSTINCT 1:  Retreat to the old belief.
             "This is too uncomfortable. Let me go back to
              what I used to believe. At least that was
              stable."

INSTINCT 2:  Grab any new belief that happens to be nearby.
             "I can't stand not knowing. Let me adopt the
              first plausible-sounding replacement so this
              can be over."
```

Both of those shortcut the process. Both leave you with a belief you haven't earned.

**The honest move in Crisis is to stay in it.** Let the old belief be clearly broken without rushing to fix it. Let the new one form slowly, from actual evidence, not from the panic of needing *some* belief to hold onto. The in-between is not a problem to solve. The in-between is where the actual work gets done.

A season of transition is uncomfortable by design. You cannot wear your coat but you cannot leave the house without one yet. That is what growing looks like from the inside.

---

## Finding the stage you're in

Pick one belief about yourself that has been loud lately. Not necessarily a sentence you say out loud, but one you can hear running underneath your decisions.

```
The belief:
__________________________________________________

Which stage is it in right now?

[ ] NORMAL      It's just true. I don't question it.

[ ] DRIFT       It still mostly runs me, but little
                things lately haven't fit. Something
                feels off.

[ ] CRISIS      It isn't working any more, and I don't
                have a replacement. Everything feels
                wobbly in this part of my life.

[ ] REVOLUTION  A new version is forming. I'm trying it
                out quietly, comparing it against the old
                one.

[ ] PARADIGM    The new version has won. The old one is
                slowly disappearing from my decisions.
```

Knowing the stage changes what the right move is.

- **In Normal:** gently poke at it. Ask whether it is actually true, or whether you have just never questioned it. Early curiosity costs almost nothing.
- **In Drift:** notice what doesn't fit any more and name it. Write it down. Don't force a conclusion.
- **In Crisis:** don't retreat and don't grab. Sit in the in-between. Let the new version form from honest experience, not from your discomfort.
- **In Revolution:** try the new belief on. Actually act from it. See whether it holds up under real conditions.
- **In Paradigm:** notice that you have moved. Give the old belief a quiet goodbye. It served you once.

---

## The one line

If you keep nothing else from this post, keep this:

> Beliefs don't die on a Tuesday. They go through a fifth season.

Whatever you have been quietly living through, the thing that doesn't quite fit any more, the ground that feels moved, the question you can't yet answer, is probably not a crisis in the dramatic sense. It is a transition. **The transition is not something that is happening to you. It is how a mind actually changes.** Let it take the time it needs. The new season will arrive.
