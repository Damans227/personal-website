Here is a list of things people walk around with. Pick the ones that sound a bit like you, or a bit like somebody you know:

- *"I need to be in control."*
- *"I have to be perfect."*
- *"I'm scared of what's coming."*
- *"Nobody really likes me."*
- *"It's not fair."*
- *"I can't take a break until it's all done."*
- *"I have to keep everyone happy."*
- *"There's no point in trying."*
- *"I don't deserve good things."*
- *"If people really saw me, they'd leave."*

They look like ten different problems. They often aren't. They are frequently one belief wearing ten costumes.

---

## The root and the branches

Imagine the mind as a tree. Up top, visible to you and to everyone else, are the branches: your behaviors, your reactions, your anxieties, the specific things you say and do that make life harder than it needs to be. Those are what you notice. Those are what you try to fix.

Down below, hidden, is the root. One or two beliefs about yourself, usually formed very young, that nobody around you knows are there. Including, often, you.

```
                "I'm not       "I need to    "Nobody        "I have to
                 safe"         be perfect"    likes me"      keep people
                                                             happy"
                     \            |            |              /
                      \           |            |             /
                       \          |            |            /
                        \         |            |           /
                         +--------+------------+----------+
                                        |
                                        v
                          +---------------------------+
                          |   "I'm not good enough"   |     <- the root
                          +---------------------------+
```

That is the belief most people's branches trace back to. Not always that one, but very often that one. *"I'm not enough."* *"I'm not sufficient as I am."* *"There is something wrong with me and I have to compensate."*

Once that belief is installed, the branches basically grow themselves. Of course you need to be perfect. If you weren't perfect, people would see the thing underneath. Of course you need to control everything. If you don't control it, the badness underneath will leak out. Of course you can't take a break. Breaks are what people who deserve rest get. Of course you have to keep everyone happy. If anyone's unhappy with you, it confirms the thing you already suspect.

**Each branch feels like its own problem.** You might spend years working on your perfectionism, or your people-pleasing, or your fear of the future, and make real progress on that one branch, only to find a new one has grown in its place. That is because the root is still intact.

---

## Why this matters

If you treat each branch as a standalone issue, you are playing whack-a-mole for the rest of your life. There are always more branches. A tree with a healthy root will keep producing them faster than you can prune them.

If, on the other hand, you can locate the root, you have a shot at actually changing the shape of the tree. Not immediately. Not easily. But the leverage point is a thousand times better.

**The question is not "what are my ten problems."** The question is "which one belief about myself, planted a long time ago, is quietly growing all ten of them."

---

## Where the root got planted

Here is the thing that is slightly uncomfortable. The root was almost certainly planted when you were very young. Three, four, five, six years old. By something that would look trivial to an adult.

A child spilled a glass of water and got shouted at. A child drew on the wrong piece of paper and someone laughed. A child was told, repeatedly, that other kids were cleverer or neater or more polite. A child reached for a parent who was tired and busy and the parent said *not now*, and the child, with no other information to go on, decided that the problem was them.

Children do not conclude "my parent was stressed." Children conclude *"I did something wrong, so I must be wrong."* That is the kind of mind a four-year-old has. The verdict goes in. The reason it went in gets forgotten. The verdict stays.

Thirty years later you are a grown person who cannot take a day off work, and you have no idea it is because of a glass of water in 1992.

## Finding your own root

This is a self-guided exercise. Give it half an hour. Pen and paper, somewhere quiet, no phone. You can do it in your head but writing it down works better.

```
HOW PRESENT IS YOUR PAST?

1. What is the earliest memory you can clearly recall from
   your childhood? One specific event, not a general feeling.

2. In that memory, what are the impressions you have of
   yourself? (Small? Scared? Bad? Alone? In trouble?)

3. What are the impressions you have of the other people
   in that memory? (Angry? Disappointed? Busy? Absent?)

4. How might that memory have shaped the course of your
   life since then? What patterns do you notice?

5. How might that experience still be shaping how you
   relate to YOURSELF today?

6. How might that experience still be shaping how you
   relate to OTHER PEOPLE today?
```

The point of question 1 is not that the earliest memory is magically the formative one. The point is that whichever memory your mind reaches for first is probably loaded. Minds don't surface neutral memories. They surface the ones that are still live.

The point of questions 5 and 6 is this. **Your past isn't finished.** It is still running the show through the beliefs it installed. The exercise is just a way of making the connection visible.

Most people, when they do this honestly, find a line that goes something like: *"Oh. The thing I decided about myself at age five is the thing I'm still trying to prove or disprove at age thirty-five."* The specifics vary. The shape doesn't.

---

## The one line

If you keep nothing else from this post, keep this:

> Ten problems can be one belief wearing ten costumes. Find the root, and the branches start to make sense.

Not every surface problem is its own thing. Sometimes they all point down.
